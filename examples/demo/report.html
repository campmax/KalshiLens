What the terminal can tell you

The public book contains YES bids and NO bids. A NO bid at $0.62 supplies a YES ask at $0.38. The explorer reconstructs both ask ladders, then walks their displayed quantities to estimate execution cost. A top quote only describes the amount available at that price.

Price versus probability

A 40¢ quote is a market price. Expected value needs an independently justified probability. The scenario panel lets you supply that assumption and vary its uncertainty. Its starting value is an illustration, not an algorithmic forecast. It does not learn the true probability from the order book.

Cost and fees

Each price level is modeled as one taker fill. Series fee metadata and event overrides determine whether the quadratic fee estimate is supported. Fees round to microdollars; balances align to the selected account precision, with an order-level rounding accumulator. Real fill fragmentation, account discounts and fee changes can differ. Unknown or flat fee schedules do not receive invented fee estimates.

Thin liquidity and price movement

Wide spreads, shallow depth and imbalanced queues describe fragility. They do not establish that someone is manipulating a market. Snapshot comparisons show observed changes; they cannot identify intent, hidden orders or which cancellations occurred between samples.

Structural relationships

If at most one of N markets can resolve YES, a basket of N NO contracts has a theoretical minimum payout of N−1 dollars under ordinary binary settlement. “Mutually exclusive” does not imply “exhaustive,” so this app does not assume buying all YES outcomes pays $1. An implication check is permitted only when your configuration explicitly marks its settlement relationship as reviewed. Exceptional settlement can invalidate a binary payout assumption.

Snapshot limitations

REST observations arrive sequentially. The quote age and maximum cross-leg time difference matter. No API key is needed, no account data is read and no orders are placed. Refreshing this HTML rereads the last saved report; it does not fetch a new market snapshot.
